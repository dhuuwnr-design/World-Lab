# WORLD LAB social network model

The next layer after cultural context is the relationship graph. Human outcomes should emerge from households, peers, workplaces, communities and institutions rather than one country-level parameter.

The current SocialNetwork stores directed sparse ties with strength, trust, contact frequency and a family flag. Household members receive strong reciprocal ties. Ties strengthen with co-location and decay when contact is absent. Perceived respect receives a small local peer signal based on tie strength, trust and the other person's social state.

This is a mechanism scaffold, not a claim that these coefficients reproduce real societies. Future versions need friendship, coworkers, school peers, mentors, online ties, community membership, migration, relationship formation/dissolution, conflict, reciprocity and calibrated network structures.

Respect is contextual and subjective. A high-status occupation can increase respect for one person and be irrelevant to another. Future reputation should emerge from observable actions, gossip/information, institutions, achievement, wealth, occupation, family reputation and social comparison.
