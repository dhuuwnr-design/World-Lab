# Individual Decision Model

WORLD LAB needs people who respond to circumstances rather than randomly selecting actions. This is an initial mechanism, not a calibrated theory of human behavior.

A decision combines goals and values, resources, constraints, perceived benefits/costs/risks, social fit and pressure, information quality, risk tolerance and uncertainty aversion. The result exposes probabilities and utilities so experiments can inspect uncertainty.

The model does not assign fixed behavior to a country, class, gender, age, religion, or culture. Those factors may influence context, values, social networks and constraints only through evidence-backed mechanisms. Family/community pressure should eventually represent reference groups, socioeconomic circumstances, local norms and individual values rather than a universal national coefficient.

Future inputs include household finances, employment, education, relationships, family approval, culture and norms, reputation, social networks, health, life stage, institutions, media/information, memory, past experience, personality, values and major life events. Choices should feed back through migration, education, employment, consumption, relationships and technology adoption.

Before claiming realistic behavior, each mechanism needs: Evidence → mechanism → calibration → simulation → validation → uncertainty. Current coefficients are provisional.
